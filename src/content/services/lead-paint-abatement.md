## What Lead Paint Abatement actually involves

Any home built before 1978, when the federal government banned lead-based residential paint, is a candidate for lead-based coatings on trim, siding, window components, doors, and baseboards. Abatement is different from a quick scrape-and-repaint: it's a regulated process under EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule, and it requires verified testing before anyone touches a painted surface. Work typically includes XRF or lab-based paint testing, plastic sheeting and negative-air containment around the work zone, wet-scraping or chemical stripping instead of dry sanding, HEPA-filtered vacuum cleanup, and a final clearance check before the area is released back to the household. Depending on scope, a single window or doorway can take a day; a full exterior repaint on an older property can run a week or more.

## Our process

1. **Lead paint testing** - XRF testing or paint-chip sampling on suspect surfaces (trim, sills, doors, painted cabinetry) to confirm lead content before any disturbance occurs.
2. **Containment setup** - poly sheeting, taped seams, and negative-air equipment isolate the work area so dust and chips don't migrate into living space or the yard.
3. **Lead paint removal** - wet-scraping, chemical stripping, or controlled component replacement, depending on what the substrate and paint condition allow; dry sanding and open-flame burning are avoided because they aerosolize lead dust.
4. **HEPA cleanup and waste handling** - HEPA vacuuming of the containment zone, wet-wiping of all surfaces, and disposal of waste per hazardous-material handling requirements.
5. **Clearance verification** - a post-work check of dust wipe samples or visual clearance (depending on scope) before containment comes down and the space is cleared for normal use.

## What separates a good lead paint abatement response from a bad one

The most common mistake on older Bakersfield homes is treating lead paint like a normal paint prep job: dry sanding, open scraping without containment, or skipping testing because "the house looks fine." None of that tells you what's actually in the paint film, and none of it stops lead dust from settling into HVAC returns or exterior soil. A lead-safe crew tests first, documents the containment setup with photos, and uses wet methods specifically because dry sanding is one of the fastest ways to cross-contaminate a property. On the paperwork side, insurers and inspectors want to see chain-of-custody on any lab samples, a clearance report, and disposal documentation for the waste stream; abatement done without that paper trail is hard to verify after the fact, which matters if a future buyer, tenant, or health department asks for proof the work was done correctly.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Bakersfield's housing stock includes a meaningful share of homes built well before the 1978 lead paint ban, especially in older established neighborhoods near the city's core. Summer heat can make exterior paint film brittle and more prone to flaking and chalking, which increases the amount of loose paint chips around foundations and window wells, something worth noting if you have young children or pets who spend time in the yard. Interior work can proceed year-round, but exterior abatement is generally easier to contain and schedule outside of peak wind days, which can carry dust further than expected.
