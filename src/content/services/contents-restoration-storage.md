## What Contents Restoration & Storage actually involves

When a pipe bursts behind a wall or smoke rolls through a living room, the structure usually gets all the attention. But the sofa, the box of tax records, the kids' baseball cards, and grandma's china cabinet are often the part of the loss a homeowner cares about most, and the part that's easiest for a crew in a hurry to write off as a total loss. Contents restoration is the itemized, documented process of removing belongings from an affected property, cleaning or restoring what can be saved, and storing everything securely until the home is ready to receive it back.

The work ranges from soft contents (clothing, bedding, upholstery) to hard contents (furniture, electronics, artwork) to documents and photographs, each of which calls for a different cleaning method. Smoke-damaged textiles may need ultrasonic cleaning or ozone deodorization. Water-affected wood furniture often needs controlled drying before refinishing is even considered. Electronics exposed to soot or moisture get assessed by a specialist before anyone plugs them back in. A full pack-out of a mid-size home can run anywhere from a day to a week depending on volume, and climate-controlled storage can run for weeks or months while the structural side of the claim is resolved.

## Our process

1. **Room-by-room inventory and photo documentation.** Before anything leaves the property, every item is logged, photographed, and tagged. This inventory becomes the paper trail your adjuster uses to verify loss and settle the claim.
2. **Triage by salvageability.** Items are sorted into clean-and-restore, deep-clean-and-restore, and non-salvageable categories, based on material type and the nature of the contamination (clean water versus sewage, smoke versus fire char, mold growth versus surface soiling).
3. **Pack-out and transport.** Salvageable contents are boxed or wrapped using material-appropriate packing (acid-free paper for documents, padded wrap for furniture, anti-static bags for electronics) and moved to a secure facility.
4. **Specialized cleaning.** Each category gets the appropriate treatment: ultrasonic immersion for hard goods, wet or dry cleaning for textiles, ozone or thermal fogging for odor, and digital recovery for electronics and photos where possible.
5. **Climate-controlled storage and pack-back.** Restored items are stored until the structure passes final inspection, then returned and placed according to the original inventory, with a final reconciliation against the initial documentation.

## What separates a good contents restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake is skipping the inventory step or doing it after items have already been moved. Without a documented chain of custody, an adjuster has no way to verify what existed before the loss, what was damaged, and what was restored versus replaced, which slows down or shrinks a claim. A rushed crew will also lump unlike materials together in transport (wet textiles against dry electronics, for instance), which can spread contamination or cause secondary damage.

Good technicians separate by material and contamination category from the first box packed, use EPA-registered antimicrobials where mold or sewage is a factor, and keep a running digital inventory that the homeowner and the adjuster can both reference. They also know when something genuinely isn't salvageable, rather than storing it indefinitely or charging to clean something that should be scoped as a loss. Adjusters generally look for itemized pre- and post-restoration photos, a clear salvage-versus-loss rationale per item, and storage invoices that match the timeline of the structural repair, not an open-ended storage bill.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Bakersfield's dry summers mean heat and dust are frequent factors in content loss, particularly when attic or garage storage areas are affected by a roof leak or HVAC failure, since dust and heat accelerate deterioration of paper goods and certain fabrics once they're wet. Winter storms bring the occasional burst pipe in older Kern County homes with galvanized plumbing, which can affect contents in lower cabinets and closets before anyone notices standing water. Because of the valley's heat, any textiles or paper goods pulled from a loss site benefit from prompt climate-controlled storage rather than sitting in a garage or trailer, where temperature swings encourage mold growth and warping.
