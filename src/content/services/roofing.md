A roof that's losing granules by the handful in the gutters, showing daylight through the attic decking, or has outlived its shingles by a decade isn't a patch-and-wait situation. It's a replacement decision, and putting it off usually means the next wind event or heavy rain turns a roofing problem into a water damage problem. ProRestoration Services handles roofing installation and replacement for Bakersfield homes and commercial buildings, from full tear-offs on aging stucco-era ranch homes to new construction installs, under the same license and crews that handle the water damage repairs when a bad roof finally fails.

## What roofing installation and replacement actually involves

A full roof replacement starts with tear-off: stripping existing shingles, underlayment, and flashing down to the deck. Once the deck is exposed, it gets inspected board by board for rot, delamination, or insect damage, and any compromised sheathing gets replaced before anything new goes down. From there it's a new underlayment layer (synthetic or felt, depending on the roof system and local code), ice and water shield at valleys and eaves where applicable, drip edge, flashing around every penetration (vents, skylights, chimneys), and finally the roofing material itself, whether that's architectural shingles, tile, or a low-slope membrane system for flat and commercial roofs. On a standard single-family home this runs one to three days depending on roof size, pitch, and material; tile and commercial membrane jobs take longer.

## Our process

1. **Roof inspection and scope.** A crew walks the roof and attic, documents deck condition, flashing points, ventilation, and any existing leak damage, and puts together a written scope with material options.
2. **Material selection and permitting.** Homeowner picks shingle, tile, or membrane type and color; ProRestoration Services pulls the required permit and schedules the tear-off date.
3. **Tear-off and deck repair.** Old roofing comes off down to the sheathing, damaged decking gets replaced, and the deck is swept and inspected before anything new is installed.
4. **Underlayment, flashing, and installation.** New underlayment, flashing, and the finish roofing material go on per manufacturer specs, with attention to valleys, penetrations, and ventilation.
5. **Final walkthrough and cleanup.** Magnetic nail sweep of the yard and driveway, gutter check, and a final inspection against the original scope before the job closes out.

## What separates a good roofing response from a bad one

The most common failure point on a re-roof isn't the shingles, it's what's underneath them. Crews that skip a real deck inspection, nail through wet or spongy sheathing instead of replacing it, and leave compromised flashing around chimneys and skylights are setting up a leak that won't show up until the next storm, by which point it's framed as a new claim instead of a workmanship issue. Ventilation gets missed too: an attic without adequate intake and exhaust venting will bake shingles from underneath and void manufacturer warranties regardless of how well the roof was installed. Insurance adjusters evaluating storm or wind damage claims look closely at whether shingles were lifted and sealed back down versus properly replaced, and whether flashing was reused instead of renewed, so documentation of what was actually removed and installed matters as much as the install itself.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Bakersfield summers push roof surface temperatures well past what most people expect, which accelerates shingle aging faster than in cooler climates and makes proper attic ventilation a bigger factor in roof lifespan than it is elsewhere. The occasional heavy winter rain and Kern River valley wind events are what actually expose a roof's weak points, usually around flashing and older, sun-brittled shingles, so late-summer or fall scheduling for a replacement avoids the scramble of fixing a failing roof mid-storm.
