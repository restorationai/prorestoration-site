## What Biohazard Cleanup actually involves

Biohazard remediation is governed by OSHA's bloodborne pathogen standard and EPA guidelines for infectious material handling, not general cleaning practice. Our technicians work in full PPE, including coveralls, respirators, and gloves rated for bloodborne pathogen exposure, and use EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants alongside enzymatic treatments that break down biological material at a molecular level. Porous materials such as carpet, padding, and in some cases subflooring or drywall often cannot be fully decontaminated and are removed and disposed of as regulated waste. Timeline depends heavily on the scope of the affected area and whether contamination has migrated into flooring or wall cavities, but most single-room jobs move from assessment to clearance within one to three days.

## Our process

1. **PPE staging and scene containment.** Before anyone enters, we seal off the affected area and suit up according to bloodborne pathogen protocol to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms.
2. **Bio-contaminant removal.** Visible biological material and any non-salvageable porous materials are removed and bagged as regulated medical waste.
3. **Enzymatic and disinfectant treatment.** Remaining surfaces are treated with enzymatic cleaners to break down residual biological matter, followed by an EPA-registered disinfectant application.
4. **Sharps and secondary hazard check.** We inspect for needles, broken glass, or other sharps that may have been overlooked, since these present a distinct exposure risk beyond fluid contamination.
5. **Waste manifest and disposal documentation.** All regulated waste is logged, transported, and disposed of through a licensed medical waste handler, with documentation provided for your records.

## What separates a good biohazard response from a bad one

The most common failure in biohazard cleanup is treating it like a deep clean rather than a regulated remediation. Surface disinfecting without removing saturated porous materials leaves contamination trapped beneath the visible layer, which can resurface as odor or health concerns weeks later. Another frequent gap is sharps handling: a scene can look clear to an untrained eye while still containing a hazard in bedding, upholstery, or flooring seams. Insurance adjusters reviewing a biohazard claim typically want a clear chain of custody for disposed materials and a waste manifest showing the material was transported by a licensed handler, not just an invoice for cleaning labor. Crews that skip documentation often cause delays or denials further into the claims process.
