## What Emergency Board-Up and Tarping actually involves

Board-up work means fitting exterior-grade plywood over broken windows, damaged doors, and compromised wall sections, fastened to framing (not just trim) so the panel holds under wind load. Roof tarping means stretching reinforced poly sheeting over a damaged section of roof, battened down with furring strips and screws rather than loose rocks or tape, so it survives a wind event instead of peeling back on the first gust. Both are temporary measures meant to stop active intrusion of water, wildlife, smoke odor, or unauthorized entry while a permanent repair or full reconstruction scope gets arranged. On most calls we're on the property within hours of the request, since a few hours of exposure can turn a roof leak into a ceiling collapse or a broken window into a soaked interior wall cavity.

## Our process

1. **Site safety check.** Before anyone climbs a ladder or steps onto a roof, we check for structural instability, exposed electrical, or fire-weakened framing, since a board-up crew's first job is not getting hurt on a compromised structure.
2. **Measure and size the opening.** Windows, doors, and roof penetrations each call for different materials, we measure the actual opening (not the visible gap) so the panel or tarp overlaps sound structure on all sides.
3. **Install board-up panels.** Plywood gets cut, fitted, and lagged into structural framing (studs, header, or roof decking), never just screwed into drywall or trim, so the panel resists wind and forced re-entry.
4. **Install roof tarping.** Poly sheeting is run past the damaged area onto sound roof deck, folded and battened with wood strips screwed through the tarp into the deck, then checked for low spots where water could pool.
5. **Document and photograph.** Every opening, panel, and tarp gets photographed before and after, which becomes part of the insurance file and the scope for permanent repair.

## What separates a good board-up response from a bad one

The most common failure point is attachment: panels screwed into trim or stucco instead of framing pull loose in the next wind event, and tarps held down with cinder blocks or duct tape shift or tear within days. A good crew also checks for hidden damage before covering an opening, a tarp stretched over a roof without checking the decking underneath can seal moisture against wood that's already saturated, which accelerates rot instead of stopping it. Adjusters reviewing a board-up claim look for dated photos of the damage before covering, proof the opening was fully secured (not just partially covered), and a clear line between temporary mitigation and the permanent repair scope, since insurers treat these as separate line items.

Bakersfield's wind events, particularly in late winter and early spring, drive most of the roof tarping calls we see, along with the occasional summer thunderstorm that rolls off the Sierra with enough gusts to peel back loose shingles.
