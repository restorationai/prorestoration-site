## When do you call a roofer, and when do you call a restoration company?

Call a restoration company when the roof damage already let water inside, or when you need an emergency tarp before the next storm front moves through; call a standalone roofer only when the roof itself is the entire job and nothing inside the house is wet.

Most Bakersfield roof calls fall into one of three buckets. A windstorm or [tree limb that fell across the roofline](/blog/tree-fell-on-house-what-to-do/) needs tarping fast, because an open roof deck plus a surprise tule-fog drizzle or a late-season thunderstorm turns a repair job into a ceiling-drywall and insulation job overnight. A slow leak, often from a cracked boot around an evaporative cooler penetration (common on older Oleander, Westchester, and Rosedale-fringe roofs) is less urgent but still worth a same-week call before the next 100-degree summer bakes the decking and the next winter rain finds the gap. A fallen power line, active electrical arcing, or a tree still resting unstably on the structure is a 911 and utility-company call first; restoration crews come in once the site is safe.

If the damage involves a downed line or structural collapse risk, do not approach the roof yourself. Everything else, from a missing shingle patch to a full tear-off after hail, is squarely inside what a licensed restoration and roofing contractor handles in one dispatch.

## What happens when you call ProRestoration for roof damage?

The first call is a quick triage: what happened, is water actively entering the house right now, and is anyone in danger. From there a technician is dispatched to assess the roof, install an emergency tarp or board-up if the deck is exposed, and check the attic and ceilings below for moisture before it shows up as a stain or a sag.

Have your policy number and the insurance company's claims line handy if you have one; ProRestoration documents the damage with photos and moisture readings that support a [storm damage claim](/blog/storm-damage-to-roof/), and provides a written scope before any repair work starts. For storm-driven losses, most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental damage like wind-lifted shingles or a tree limb strike; ongoing leaks from deferred maintenance are usually treated differently, so it is worth checking your policy language or reviewing how the [Insurance Information Institute explains covered perils](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance). Once the roof is stabilized, the crew either completes the repair or, for larger losses, schedules a full replacement.
