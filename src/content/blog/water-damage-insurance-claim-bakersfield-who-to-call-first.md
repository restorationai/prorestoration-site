## Who do I call first after water damage in Bakersfield?

Call whoever can physically stop the water first, then a restoration company for mitigation, then your insurer. If the source is an active leak, that's a plumber or your main shutoff valve. If the water has already stopped (a one-time appliance failure, a pipe that already drained), the restoration call comes first.

In Bakersfield, the most common sudden losses are slab leaks and burst supply lines, a direct result of slab-on-grade construction and some of the hardest water in the Central Valley eating through copper and galvanized pipe over time. If the leak is still running under the slab or behind a wall, the water has to be shut off before drying equipment does any good. Once the source is controlled, the restoration call is the one that actually protects your claim. [Standing water left untreated](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) for more than 24 to 48 hours gives mold a real foothold, and insurers expect documented, prompt mitigation as part of your duty to limit the loss.

## What should I do before the insurance adjuster arrives?

Document everything before any tear-out begins: wide shots of every affected room, close-ups of the source, and a few seconds of video narrating what happened and when you noticed it. Keep every receipt from the moment water touched a wall.

A restoration crew doing this correctly will take moisture readings at the baseboard, mid-wall, and subfloor before removing a single piece of drywall, and photograph each step of demolition. That moisture-mapped record, not a verbal description after the fact, is what an adjuster uses to verify the scope of the loss and approve the estimate. Ask for a written scope and an itemized estimate, room by room, with square footage and the equipment deployed, not a one-line lump-sum number.

If you're unsure what your specific policy requires, the California Department of Insurance publishes a [Residential Property Claims Guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/03-res/res-prop-claim.cfm) and runs a consumer hotline at 1-800-927-4357 for homeowners who want a neutral second opinion on how a claim should be handled.

## Will my homeowners insurance cover this leak?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from inside the home, like a burst supply line, a failed water heater, or a pipe that lets go without warning. What they typically exclude is gradual damage: a slow seep behind a wall, long-term condensation, or deferred maintenance that let a small drip become a big problem.

That distinction, sudden versus gradual, is usually the single biggest factor in whether a claim gets approved. A slab leak that's been weeping for months before anyone noticed a warm spot on the floor can get more scrutiny than a pipe that split overnight. [Coverage details vary by carrier and policy](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance), so no restoration company, including ours, can promise your claim will be paid. What we can do is document the loss the way adjusters expect, which gives you the strongest footing either way.
