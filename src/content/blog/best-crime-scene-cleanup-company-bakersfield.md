## Who Decides When a Crime Scene Can Be Cleaned?

Law enforcement or the Kern County Coroner's Office must release the scene before any cleanup company can legally enter. No restoration crew, however qualified, can start before that release happens.

This release usually comes after evidence collection is finished, which can take anywhere from several hours to a couple of days depending on the case. Once released, the property owner or a family member can authorize cleanup. A good company will ask whether the scene has been released as the first question on the call, not an afterthought.

## Does Insurance or CalVCB Cover Crime Scene Cleanup in California?

Most homeowners and renters insurance policies cover crime scene and trauma cleanup under the same provisions that cover sudden, accidental property damage, though coverage and deductibles vary by policy. Always confirm the specifics with your insurer before assuming full reimbursement.

For victims and families who don't have applicable insurance, California's Victim Compensation Board can help cover cleanup costs tied to a qualifying crime. A company experienced with these claims will walk you through both paths rather than leaving you to figure it out while grieving. ## How Long Does Crime Scene Cleanup Take?

Most single-room trauma cleanups are completed within one day once the scene is released; larger scenes involving multiple rooms, flooring removal, or structural materials can take several days. The scope depends on what was affected, not just the square footage.

Contaminated porous materials like carpet, padding, and some drywall are usually removed rather than cleaned, since blood and other bodily fluids can't be fully decontaminated from absorbent surfaces. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned and disinfected in place. A written scope before work begins tells you exactly what's being removed, what's being treated, and why.

## What Should You Ask Before You Hire a Cleanup Company?

Ask five questions before you authorize any work: Are you licensed with the CSLB? Is your team trained under OSHA's bloodborne pathogen standard? Who hauls the medical waste, and are they state-approved? Will I get a written estimate before work starts? And can you help with insurance or CalVCB paperwork?

A company that answers all five without hesitation is one you can trust with this kind of job. One that can't name its license number or explain where the waste goes is a company to pass on.
