If you unlocked your office Monday morning to soaked carpet tile, water dripping through a ceiling tile, and a desktop monitor sitting in an inch of standing water, the loss likely started sometime over the weekend. In Bakersfield, the usual suspects are a burst supply line, a failed water heater in the breakroom, or a sprinkler head that discharged while the building sat empty. The order you make calls in over the next hour matters almost as much as how fast you make them.

## Who Do You Call First for a Flooded Office in Bakersfield?

Call a water damage restoration company before you call your insurance agent, and only call a plumber separately if water is still actively running from a source you cannot shut off yourself.

A restoration crew brings extraction equipment, dehumidifiers and the documentation an insurer needs, all in one visit. ProRestoration Services runs a 24/7 line for Bakersfield and Kern County commercial accounts, so after-hours and weekend discoveries don't have to wait until Monday for extraction to start. If the source is a fixture that's still leaking, a plumber or your maintenance contractor can stop the flow while the restoration crew sets up.

A sprinkler discharge is a special case. Only shut the riser valve yourself if someone on staff is actually trained on the building's fire suppression system. Closing the wrong valve can disable protection for the rest of the building, so if no one is certain, wait for the fire department or a licensed fire-protection contractor to handle the shutoff while everyone else focuses on the water already on the floor.

## What Should You Do in the First Hour, Before Anyone Arrives?

The first hour is about stopping the damage from spreading and documenting what you have, not starting cleanup.

1. **Shut off the water source** if you can reach a valve safely, whether that's the building's main or an appliance supply line.
2. **Cut power at the breaker panel** to any circuit serving the wet area. Don't unplug anything that's sitting in standing water.
3. **Keep staff and customers out** of the affected rooms until the floor has been checked for electrical hazards.
4. **Photograph everything before you move it**, floors, ceiling tiles, furniture, and equipment, from several angles.
5. **Write down the time you found it** and anything you noticed that might explain the cause.

Most commercial supply-line and appliance leaks start as clean water (what the [IICRC's S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) calls Category 1), but that changes the longer it sits or the more building material it touches. Don't assume a puddle that's been there since Friday is still safe to walk through without gloves.

## How Do You Protect Computers, Files, and Inventory While It Dries?

Turn off wet electronics and leave them off; don't try to dry them with a fan or power them back on to "test" them, since that can finish off a board that water alone didn't ruin.

A contents restoration crew inventories what's affected, photographs it for the claim, and sorts equipment and furniture into what can be cleaned in place, what needs to be packed out and dried at a facility, and what's a loss. Paper records, invoices, client files, can sometimes be recovered through specialized drying rather than assumed destroyed, which matters if you're short on backups. Keeping that inventory organized from hour one is also what turns into your documentation if you're filing a business interruption claim later.

## Can the Office Stay Open During Drying, and What About Insurance?

Most offices can keep operating in the rooms that weren't affected while drying equipment runs in the flooded zone, as long as the crew sets up containment and the power is safe in the dry areas.

Restoration crews commonly dry room by room or zone by zone on a commercial job specifically so the front desk, a conference room, or half the floor can stay in use. Ask the crew what the noise and airflow plan looks like for your business hours versus overnight.

On the coverage side, most commercial property policies pay for sudden, accidental water damage like a burst supply line or a sprinkler malfunction. Slow leaks from deferred maintenance are simply excluded and no add-on fixes that. Rising water from outside the building is flood damage, which typically requires a separate flood insurance policy rather than an endorsement on your existing policy, so read your policy's water damage section before you assume you're covered. If your policy includes business interruption coverage, the inventory and timeline the restoration crew documents is often exactly what the insurer asks for ([more on claim order here](/blog/water-damage-insurance-claim-bakersfield-who-to-call-first/)).
