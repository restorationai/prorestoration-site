## What Should You Check Before Hiring a Fire Damage Company in Bakersfield?

Confirm three things before any crew starts demolition: an active CSLB license, drying and cleaning work that follows the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/), and a written scope of work you can hand to your insurance adjuster.

You can verify any contractor's license status directly through the [CSLB license lookup tool](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx) before you sign anything. A written scope matters because fire claims involve structural repair, contents cleaning, and sometimes temporary housing, and insurers want line-item documentation, not a verbal estimate.

Ask, too, whether the company can handle plumbing damage caused by the fire or by the firefighting response itself. Burst or melted supply lines inside a wall cavity are common after a structure fire, and coordinating that repair separately from the restoration work adds delay. For background on vetting any contractor in this market, see [How To Choose a Restoration Company in Bakersfield](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/).

## What Happens After a House Fire in Bakersfield?

After the fire department clears the home, the first job is securing the structure: boarding broken windows and tarping a damaged roof section so wind doesn't cause a second loss overnight. From there, a certified crew removes charred debris, runs HEPA air scrubbers to pull soot particulate out of the air, and cleans or discards smoke-damaged contents depending on how deep the char and odor have set in.

Kern County's dry, 100-degree-plus summers push electrical-fire frequency up from overloaded AC and evaporative cooler circuits, while winter tule fog season brings a wave of space-heater house fires, so the pattern a Bakersfield crew sees shifts with the season. For a fuller walkthrough of the first 24 hours, see [What to Do After a House Fire in Bakersfield, CA](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-bakersfield/) and [The Fire Damage Restoration Process, Explained](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/). The [American Red Cross' guide on what to do after a home fire](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/fire/home-fire-recovery.html) covers the immediate safety steps before any contractor arrives.
