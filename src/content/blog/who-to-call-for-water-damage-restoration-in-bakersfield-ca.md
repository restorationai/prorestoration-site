## When to Call Someone Else Before the Restoration Crew

Most water damage calls go straight to a restoration company. But a few situations require a different first call.

**Call 911 or your utility company first if:**
- There's any chance of electrical hazard (water near a panel, outlets, or appliances that are still energized)
- You smell gas
- Structural collapse is a concern (ceiling sagging heavily with standing water above it)

Do not enter a room with standing water until you're certain the electricity to that area is off at the breaker.

**Call a plumber first if:**
- The water source is still actively flowing and you cannot shut it off at the main
- You have a slab leak (common in Bakersfield's slab-on-grade construction with hard water) and the location is unknown

With ProRestoration and All Pro Plumbing operating as one team, you can skip this step entirely, call **(661) 393-9306)** and both problems get dispatched together.

## What Happens When You Call ProRestoration?

Knowing what to expect on that first call makes the process less stressful. Here's how it goes.

**On the phone:** Tell them the source of the water if you know it (burst pipe, appliance leak, evaporative cooler overflow, sewage backup), which rooms are affected, and whether the water source is stopped. Have your address ready. If you're not sure whether it's covered by insurance, they can walk you through that too.

**What to have ready:**
- Your address and the best way to reach you
- Your insurance carrier name and policy number (if you have it handy, not required to get started)
- Whether the electricity to the affected area is off
- A rough sense of how long the water has been there

**When the crew arrives:** A project manager will assess the loss category and class. Category 1 is clean water (supply line, water heater). Category 2 is gray water (washing machine, dishwasher). Category 3, also called black water, is sewage or flood water and requires full PPE and sanitization. The [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold) explains why Category 3 losses need professional handling: contamination spreads fast.

The crew will extract standing water, set up air movers and commercial dehumidifiers, and take baseline moisture readings with a calibrated meter. Those readings are logged every day until the structure hits dry standard, typically 3 to 5 days for a straightforward loss, longer for Category 3 or heavily saturated materials.

If the water has been sitting more than 24 to 48 hours, mold growth is already a real possibility. See [How Quickly Does Mold Grow After Water Damage?](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/) for what to watch for.
