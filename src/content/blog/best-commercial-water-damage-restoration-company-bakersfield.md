## What should a commercial water damage restoration company in Bakersfield actually have?

A qualified commercial water damage restoration company holds IICRC Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) certifications, a current CSLB contractor's license, and proof of general liability and workers' compensation insurance it can produce before a crew sets foot on your property.

Drying should follow the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry benchmark for how water losses are categorized and dried. On a commercial job that means daily moisture-mapping logs with photos, not a single verbal "it's dry now" at the end of the week. Ask to see a sample log before you sign anything. You can also [verify any California contractor's license directly through the CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx) in under a minute, which is worth doing before a crew unloads equipment.

Square footage matters too. A company that has mostly dried bedrooms and living rooms may not have the air-mover and dehumidifier capacity to handle a 15,000-square-foot warehouse floor or a multi-tenant office suite. Ask directly how many jobs of similar size they've completed in the past year.

## What should you ask a commercial restoration contractor before hiring?

A short list of direct questions tells you more about a contractor's commercial readiness than any marketing page. Ask these before you sign a work authorization:

- Have you handled a loss at this square footage before, and can you name the property or give a reference?
- Who on your crew holds WRT and ASD certification, and can you provide their certificate numbers?
- Will drying progress be documented with daily moisture logs and photos I can access?
- Do you write estimates in Xactimate, and will you coordinate line items directly with our adjuster?
- Can mitigation and reconstruction happen under one contract, or will I need a second contractor for the rebuild?
- Can your crew work nights and weekends to limit how long the space is unusable?
- Can you provide current proof of general liability and workers' compensation coverage?

A contractor that hesitates on any of these, especially the insurance proof, is worth passing on. This is also the point to ask about Xactimate specifically: it's the line-item estimating software most commercial insurers expect, and a contractor who doesn't use it will slow down your claim.

## How is a commercial water loss different from a residential one?

A commercial loss adds business-interruption pressure, multiple trades, and often a property-management layer that a homeowner's claim never has. The contractor has to manage all three at once, not just dry the building.

A failed supply line behind a break room sink isn't just a plumbing fix and it isn't just a drying job. It's both, plus whatever flooring, drywall, and ceiling tile the water touched on its way down. This is where a one-call setup earns its keep: ProRestoration Services pairs its sister company, All Pro Plumbing, to handle the plumbing repair itself, while its own mitigation and reconstruction crews start extraction and drying the same day, under one contract, under the company's "One Call Does It All" structure. No handoff between a plumber and a separate restoration company, no gap in documentation between the two.

Insurance is also different. Commercial property policies often include business-interruption coverage, and how a claim is documented affects whether that coverage pays out cleanly. The [Insurance Information Institute's guide to settling claims after a disaster](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) is a useful primer on what adjusters look for: photos, moisture readings, and a written scope, all dated. A contractor that already builds this documentation into its process protects the claim better than one that treats paperwork as an afterthought.
