## What separates a real mold remediation company from a spray-and-go outfit?

A real mold remediation company follows the structure in the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/): physical containment to stop spores from spreading to the rest of the house, negative air pressure with HEPA filtration running for the duration of the job, removal of mold-affected porous material like drywall and insulation rather than just killing surface growth with a chemical spray, and a clearance test from an independent party before the job is called done.

Containment usually means zip-wall poly sheeting sealing off the work area, with a HEPA air scrubber creating negative pressure so spores don't migrate into the rest of the home while the crew works. That matters more in a lot of Bakersfield's pre-1970 homes in Oleander, Westchester, and East Bakersfield, where failing galvanized supply lines and slab leaks push moisture behind walls for weeks before anyone notices. A surface wipe-down does nothing for growth that's established inside a wall cavity. Mold can start growing on wet material within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold), which is why the moisture source has to be fixed, not just the visible stain.

## How is mold remediation different from mold testing by the same company?

Mold remediation removes and treats existing contamination. Mold testing identifies whether contamination exists and how bad it is, usually through air samples or surface swabs sent to an independent lab for analysis. These are two different jobs, and the best setup keeps them separate.

A company that inspects your home, finds mold, and then quotes you its own remediation job with no independent lab confirmation has an incentive problem worth asking about directly. That doesn't automatically mean the finding is wrong, but you deserve a third party in the loop, especially on a job that can run into several thousand dollars. ## What should a mold remediation estimate include?

A legitimate mold remediation estimate itemizes the work by task, not just a single lump-sum number. At minimum, it should spell out:

- The containment plan and square footage being sealed off
- PPE level the crew will wear (full Tyvek, N95 or P100 respirators, for anything beyond a small, isolated patch)
- Which materials are being removed versus cleaned in place, and why
- Antimicrobial treatment on remaining structural surfaces
- Drying equipment and run-time after removal
- A line item for independent clearance testing before the containment comes down

If the moisture problem started with a plumbing failure such as a slab leak or a burst supply line, that's a separate repair the estimate should either include or clearly hand off. ## What are the red flags when hiring a mold remediation company in Bakersfield?

The biggest red flag is a same-day bid with no physical inspection and no mention of containment at all. Walk away from any of these:

- No containment or negative air pressure mentioned in the scope
- A "we'll spray it and it's gone" pitch with no material removal discussion
- No independent clearance testing offered before the job is considered finished
- Pressure to sign the same day, especially paired with a cash-only payment ask
- No license number provided, or reluctance to let you verify it

California contractors doing this kind of work need a current license, and you can verify any contractor's status directly through the [CSLB license lookup tool](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx) before you sign anything. It takes two minutes and it's the single best filter available.

## What questions should you ask before you hire?

Before you sign a contract, ask the company directly:

1. Will you set up physical containment and negative air pressure for this job?
2. What specific materials are you removing, and what are you treating in place?
3. Who runs the clearance test, and is it an independent third party?
4. What's your CSLB license number, and can I verify it myself?
5. If the moisture source is a plumbing failure, who repairs that, and is it included?

A company that answers all five without hesitation has done this before. One that gets vague on containment or clearance testing is telling you something, even if they don't say it outright.
