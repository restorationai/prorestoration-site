## What Should You Do in the First Few Minutes?

Call 911 immediately if anyone in the home needs medical attention or if the situation is still unfolding. If you or another family member is struggling with the loss itself, call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, a free and confidential service staffed around the clock.

Until law enforcement has finished its work, don't go back into the affected room or touch anything inside it, including personal belongings. It feels unbearable to wait, but it protects both the investigation and your own safety. Many families also lean on 988 or a local grief counselor in these first hours, not just for the person who was lost, but for everyone in the house trying to process what just happened.

## Who Releases the Scene, Bakersfield Police or the Coroner?

In Bakersfield, officers from the Bakersfield Police Department respond first, or Kern County Sheriff's deputies if the home is in an unincorporated area, and the Kern County Sheriff-Coroner's Office determines when the scene can be released back to the family. No cleanup, repair, or removal of items should happen before that release.

This step can take a few hours, and sometimes stretches into the next day depending on the investigation. Families are often advised to stay elsewhere, with a friend, relative, or hotel, during this window. Once the coroner's office signs off, you're legally and practically clear to bring in help.

## Why Shouldn't Family Clean This Themselves?

Blood and other bodily fluids can carry bloodborne pathogens, including hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV, and in California these materials are treated as regulated medical waste that cannot go into household trash. Standard household cleaners and store-bought disinfectants don't reach the level of decontamination this type of loss requires, and affected flooring, subflooring, or drywall often has to be removed entirely rather than wiped down.

The [OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens standard](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens) exists precisely because this kind of exposure carries real health risk for anyone without training and protective equipment. Professional trauma cleanup follows the [ANSI/IICRC S540 Standard for Trauma and Crime Scene Cleanup](https://iicrc.org/s540/), which covers everything from PPE and containment to how contaminated materials are packaged and disposed of as regulated waste. ## Who Pays for Suicide Cleanup in Bakersfield?

Most homeowners insurance policies cover trauma and biohazard cleanup after an unattended death or suicide, often under the same provision that applies to accidental damage inside the home, though every policy is different and the only way to know for certain is to call your carrier and ask directly.
