## Why Dustin Acres Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of the housing stock in and around Dustin Acres went up during the tract-building booms of the 1970s through 1990s, which means a lot of homes still run on original copper or galvanized steel supply lines that are now reaching or past their expected service life. Add in slab-on-grade construction, which is standard across this part of the Central Valley, and a pinhole leak under the slab can soak into the concrete and surrounding soil for weeks before anyone notices a damp spot on the carpet or a musty smell near the baseboards. Clay-heavy soil common to this stretch of Kern County also shrinks and swells with the seasons, which stresses slab joints and underground plumbing in ways that flatter, sandier soils don't. When winter storms finally arrive after a dry summer, that soil movement plus sudden heavy rain is a common recipe for slab leaks and foundation seepage that homeowners weren't expecting.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Dustin Acres

We start with moisture mapping, using meters to find out how far water has traveled under flooring and behind walls, since a slab leak rarely stays contained to the room where it surfaced. Standing water gets extracted first with truck-mounted or portable pumps, followed by targeted extraction from carpet pad and any cavities where water has pooled. From there we set up structural drying equipment, air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage and calibrated to the IICRC S500 standard, and we track moisture readings daily until materials return to a dry standard rather than guessing based on how a room looks or smells. For slab leaks specifically, we coordinate with a plumber early so the water source is isolated before drying equipment runs, since continued seepage under equipment just wastes time and electricity without solving the underlying problem.

One thing that catches people off guard in this part of Kern County: even though the climate is dry most of the year, tule fog season in late fall and winter brings mornings of heavy ground-level humidity that can slow down structural drying by a day or more compared to a dry August job.
