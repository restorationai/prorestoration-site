## Why Keene Properties See Water Damage Issues

Keene sits in the Caliente Creek drainage where the Tehachapi Mountains funnel winter storm runoff straight toward the pass. When a heavy rain event hits, culverts and roadside ditches along Highway 58 can back up faster than they drain, and that runoff finds its way into crawlspaces and foundations on properties built into the hillside. Many homes out here were built decades ago on raised foundations or crawlspace footings rather than slab, which means a slow leak or storm intrusion can sit under the floor joists for days before anyone notices the smell or the give in the subfloor. Well water systems add another wrinkle: a failed pressure tank or a cracked supply line can dump hundreds of gallons before a homeowner who's away from the property even knows there's a problem, since there's no municipal meter flagging unusual usage.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Keene

We start with moisture mapping, using meters to find out how far water has traveled through flooring, framing, and insulation, not just where it's visibly pooled. In Keene's older homes that often means pulling baseboards and checking wall cavities behind plaster or early drywall, since those materials hold moisture longer than modern board. Extraction comes next with truck-mount or portable pumps depending on site access, followed by placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space rather than a one-size approach. Because several Keene properties run on septic and well systems, we also check that drying equipment isn't drawing more power or water pressure than the property's infrastructure can handle, and we document everything with photos and moisture logs for your insurance file as we go, drying to the IICRC S500 standard throughout.

A number of homes in the Keene area back up to grazing land or undeveloped hillside, and after a dry summer followed by a wildfire burn scar upslope, even a moderate rain event can send ash-laden runoff toward a foundation that's never flooded before.
