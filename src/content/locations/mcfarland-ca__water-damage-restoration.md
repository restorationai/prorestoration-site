## Why McFarland Properties See Water Damage Issues

McFarland sits in the flat, clay-heavy soil of the southern San Joaquin Valley, where most residential construction since the postwar agricultural boom has relied on slab-on-grade foundations. That slab design works fine in dry years, but Central Valley clay expands when it gets wet and contracts hard when it dries, which stresses the plumbing lines running beneath the concrete. Add in a plumbing stock that, in the town's older sections, still includes galvanized steel supply lines from the mid-20th century, and you get a higher rate of pinhole leaks and slow slab leaks than you'd expect in newer subdivisions. Flood irrigation on neighboring agricultural parcels can also push groundwater levels up seasonally, which means a home's crawlspace or slab edge may already be carrying moisture before a storm or interior leak adds to it. None of this shows up as a puddle in the living room. It shows up as a baseboard that stays damp for weeks, or a faint mineral smell near an interior wall that nobody can quite place.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McFarland

We start by mapping moisture, not just chasing the visible wet spot. On slab homes, that means checking the perimeter where the slab meets the exterior wall, since that's where water migrating under the foundation tends to surface first. Extraction comes next, using truck-mounted or portable units depending on how far the water has traveled through the structure. Structural drying is where McFarland jobs often diverge from a coastal city's timeline: dense clay soil beneath a slab holds moisture longer than sandy or loam soils, so we typically run air movers and dehumidifiers on a slightly extended cycle and verify with follow-up moisture readings rather than guessing based on a standard drying calendar. Drying is tracked against the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and we document every reading for the insurance file. Reconstruction, when needed, follows once materials test dry, not before.
